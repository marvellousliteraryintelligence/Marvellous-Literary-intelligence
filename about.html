Our Story

The Story Behind Marvellous Literary Intelligence

Marvellous Literary Intelligence began with a question I could not stop asking: why do so many good books get lost in a marketplace that holds millions of titles, and why do the readers who might value them sometimes never find them?

A Lifelong Connection With Books

I grew up around books from a very young age. They were a constant presence in my life, shaping how I thought, what I noticed, and what I came to care about. Books were never background objects to me. They were worlds, voices, arguments, and companions.

Because of that connection, I developed a particular sensitivity to the books around me. I noticed when a book was good. I noticed when it was quietly brilliant. And over time, I began noticing something else: many of those books, despite their quality, were not reaching the readers who would have loved them.

Image Placeholderlibrary-shelves-editorial.png

The Frustration That Would Not Go Away

One thing has always bothered me: seeing good books get lost among millions of titles competing for attention on Amazon and across the online book marketplace. These were books with care behind them. Books with voice. Books that would mean something to the right reader. And yet, somehow, the right reader never seemed to find them.

I watched this happen again and again. I watched authors do everything they had been told to do, publish well, write well, show up, promote, and still struggle to be seen. I watched readers who, I knew, would have loved a particular book, remain unaware that it existed.

“Why does this keep happening? And why do the readers who might value a book sometimes never discover it?”

That question stayed with me. The more I asked it, the clearer it became that the usual explanations were not enough. Low visibility was not simply a sign of a weak book, a lazy author, or bad luck. Something more structural was happening, and almost nobody was looking at it closely.

Investigating Instead of Accepting

I decided to investigate the problem instead of accepting it. That decision became the foundation of Marvellous Literary Intelligence.

I started paying close attention to how books actually appear in the online marketplace: how they are listed, categorised, positioned, described, and surfaced. I studied the relationship between a book and its potential readers. I looked at the small, often invisible details that quietly decide whether a book is noticed or ignored.

What I found changed how I think about book marketing. Discoverability is rarely a single problem. It is a pattern of connected signals, listing presentation, metadata, positioning, platform, reader language, and competition, that either guide readers toward a book or allow it to disappear.

Ten Years of Experience

Work That Grew From a Question

For the past 10 years, I have helped authors address low or limited online visibility, improve how their books are presented and discovered online, and create better opportunities to reach readers. Every piece of that work grew from the same original question, and from a conviction that the question deserves serious investigation.

The work takes different shapes depending on what a book needs. Sometimes it begins with a careful look at a single listing. Sometimes it is a full strategy for a launch or a backlist. Sometimes it is an audit of the author's wider online presence, where small gaps are quietly costing visibility.

What stays the same is the approach: look closely, examine the evidence, and only then recommend what to change.

The Mission

To help close the visibility gap between books and the readers who may be interested in them.

That is what Marvellous Literary Intelligence is here to do. Not to promise bestseller lists or guaranteed sales. Not to replace the writing, the publishing team, or the author's own voice. The mission is more specific and, in some ways, more honest: to understand why a particular book is not being discovered, and to help the author decide what to do about it.

A book that cannot be found cannot be chosen. A reader who cannot find a book they would value loses something too. Closing that gap matters.

An Evidence-Led Philosophy

The way I work with authors is shaped by a few quiet convictions. These are not slogans. They are the principles that decide what I will and will not do.

Investigate Before Blaming

Low discoverability should be investigated, not automatically blamed on the book or the author. Many books labelled as failures were simply never given a fair chance to be found.

Evidence Over Opinion

Recommendations should come from research into your book's actual marketplace context, not from generic advice or whatever happens to be trending online this month.

Clarity, Not Hype

Authors are told enough hype already. What they often need instead is a calm, written assessment of what is working, what is not, and what might be worth trying next.

Respect for the Author

The author decides. My role is to surface findings and options clearly, so you can make informed choices about your book, your time, and your budget.

Honesty About Limits

I do not guarantee sales, rankings, reviews, or publicity. No one honest can. What I can offer is careful work, considered thinking, and recommendations grounded in evidence.

Care for the Reader Too

Closing the visibility gap serves readers as much as authors. A book finding its reader is one of the quietly good things a marketplace can produce.

Who I Work With

The authors I work with are varied. Some are independently published, some are traditionally published, some are hybrid. Some have one book, others have a growing catalogue. Some write fiction, others write non-fiction. What they tend to share is a specific feeling: the sense that their book deserves more visibility than it is currently receiving, and a willingness to look honestly at why.

If that sounds familiar, you are in the right place.
